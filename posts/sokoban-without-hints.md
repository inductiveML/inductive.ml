# Sokoban Without Hints

## The challenge

On Oct 17, 2026, kache [posted](https://x.com/yacineMTB/status/2111504874844127300) a suggestion: "check out Joseph Suarez's state space branches and figure out how to make the sokoban solve not take any domain specific priors and then sweep it across everything else."

In Sokoban you push boxes onto targets in a small grid. Some pushes can't be undone, so one careless move can make a level unsolvable. Boxoban is DeepMind's large set of 10×10 Sokoban levels, and PufferLib, Joseph Suarez's reinforcement learning library, ships an environment for it. Its default reward includes a hint: +0.25 each time a box lands on a target, and −1 when time runs out. That hint is a domain-specific prior. It tells the agent what progress looks like before the agent has ever finished a level.

We wanted to remove that. Our agent gets +1 for solving a level and 0 for everything else.

## The V-trace bug: a correction that kept growing

We started on Suarez's `simplecl` branch. It trains with V-trace, a way of scoring past moves when the data was collected by a slightly older version of the policy. It also replays training segments, giving priority to the ones with the largest advantage, where the model's value estimate was furthest off.

In plain terms, the trainer learns a value estimate (how good the current position is) and pulls it toward targets computed from what actually happened. In this game no episode pays more than 1, so no target should be much bigger than 1. In our runs the largest target reached 3.62e10, about 36 billion. The cause was one setting. It let a correction be multiplied by a factor above 1 at each step back through an episode, so the correction grew instead of shrinking. Prioritized replay then kept picking exactly the rows where that had happened.

The technical version: V-trace computes its targets backwards, v_t = V(x_t) + δ_t + γ c_t (v_{t+1} − V(x_{t+1})), where c_t is the importance ratio clipped at c̄. The tuned Boxoban settings used c̄ = 5 and ρ̄ = 3.09. IMPALA's analysis of V-trace assumes ρ̄ ≥ c̄ ([Espeholt et al. 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01561), §4.1), and this was outside that. With c̄ = 5, any run of steps whose ratio is above 1 multiplies the correction by γc_t > 1 per step. Prioritized replay closes the loop. A segment with a large advantage gets high priority and is resampled many times in the same epoch (the rows that started the blow-ups were resampled a median of 25 times, at most 2,104). Its ratio drifts further from 1 (median 2.03 at those rows), and the next pass multiplies again. With c̄ = 1, γc_t ≤ γ < 1 and the recursion shrinks.

Setting `vtrace_c_clip 1` fixed it. In a controlled pair of 2B-step runs that differed only in that setting, the largest target fell from 4.35e7 to 3.67, and the held-out solve rate at 2B went from 9.7% to 35.6%. Mainline PufferLib's default for this setting is already 1.

We weren't the only ones to see trouble here. In a Sep 18, 2026 post, Suarez independently dropped V-trace and prioritized replay, citing a bad interaction between prioritized sampling and higher sample reuse [unverified: we haven't located the post; link needed].

## Then the curriculum stopped mattering

Up to this point we had been building curricula. In our fullest one, the agent restarted games from saved states, rehearsed a library of levels it had already beaten, and played levels made by a level generator. With the fix on, we trained one more arm: plain training on uniformly random levels, with no curriculum at all. All of these runs used the same win-only reward, network and seed, and were scored on the same 20,000 held-out levels.

Plain training won. At 2B steps its held-out solve rate was 69.9%, against 64.3% for our full curriculum, 60.9% for a state-reset curriculum modelled on Suarez's and 54.1% for our curriculum without its level generator. A second seed of plain training reached 70.0%. The curricula helped only early: at 0.5B our full curriculum led plain training, 16.1% to 11.8%. Run longer, plain training reached 81.4% at 8B, while the curriculum run levelled off at 76.2% by 8.5B.

## The result, on official Boxoban splits

We then moved the recipe to mainline PufferLib 5.0 with a small patch. The patch adds a three-layer conv encoder in front of mainline's MinGRU core, plus two flags that switch off the timeout penalty and the random start clock. The network has 733,120 parameters. Training uses the win-only reward, no curriculum and no search. Levels are drawn uniformly from 849,981 of Boxoban's `unfiltered/train` levels, after removing any that share a layout with an evaluation level. A 3B-step run took 4.8 hours on one RTX 6000 Ada that was shared with other jobs.

On Boxoban's official `unfiltered/test` split (1,000 levels, one attempt each), the 3B model solves:

- **80.7%** with sampled actions and a 150-step limit, the setting it was trained with;
- **74.7%** greedy at 120 steps, the Sokoban Speedrun's protocol (DeepMind's DRC work also caps episodes at 120 steps).

The 8B run from the `simplecl` branch reaches 79.9% greedy at 120 steps.

## A fair comparison

The comparison that matters is M1-unf: mainline's shipped Boxoban config, trained by the same trainer on the same levels with the same seed and budget. It keeps the shaped reward (+0.25 per box onto a target, −0.25 per box off, −1 on a timeout) and its own 9.9M-parameter network. It reaches 74.4%. The hint-free model is 6.3 points higher on test with sampled actions (paired McNemar p = 1.3e-5) and 3.9 points higher greedy at 120 steps (p = 0.015). It also solves more medium levels (21.2% vs 15.3%) and hard levels (8.0% vs 5.9%).

This is a comparison of two whole configurations, not of the hint alone. Besides the reward, they differ in network, timeout penalty, start clock and advantage estimator (the shipped config doesn't use V-trace). Our model trained without V-trace still scores 79.7%, so V-trace alone doesn't explain the gap. The cleaner test of the hint by itself is in the limits section below.

Mainline's config as shipped trains on medium levels, and scores 37.2% on unfiltered test. Most of the gap between that and our 80.7% comes from the level set, not the hint: the same shaped config trained on unfiltered levels scores 74.4%. So 37.2% isn't the number to compare against.

## Other games

The second half of kache's suggestion was to sweep across other games. We did part of that. We swapped the same conv encoder into mainline's shipped configs for three other grid games, keeping each game's own reward.

- **snake:** the mean snake length at death was 118.1 and 119.1 with the conv encoder, against 104.1 and 104.4 with the default one (2 seeds). The conv arm used 3× the shipped learning rate, picked by a short learning-rate check.
- **trash_pickup:** 38.70 and 38.72 trash collected per game (of 40), against 37.51 and 37.50 (2 seeds).
- **maze:** a tie. Seeds split 2–1: final solve rates on 2,000 fresh maps were 84.17%, 84.95% and 85.78% with the conv encoder, against 84.15%, 82.25% and 87.40% with the default one.

This tests the encoder, not hint removal. These games' rewards weren't changed.

## Limits

- **At small budgets, the hint matters.** We took the Sokoban Speedrun's record setup and its 0.5B-step budget, and removed only the box hint and the timeout penalty. Hint-free training scored 0.0% and 5.8% (2 seeds), against a 72.8% average for the shaped setup (3 seeds). Win-only learning starts late. Our 3B model was at 0.45% on held-out levels at 0.5B and took off around 0.7–1B. So the hint buys a fast start, not a higher ceiling.
- **DeepMind's DRC is far ahead.** It reports 99% on unfiltered test after 1e9 steps, with a shaped reward.
- **Medium levels stay hard.** Our 3B model solves 21.2% of medium levels and 8.0% of hard ones.
- **Our hint-free DRC run never learned.** It was at 0.05% after 1B steps, with policy entropy at its maximum, and we stopped it.
- **Many results are one seed.** That includes the 3B model, the shaped baseline and the 8B run. The other-game results use 2 seeds per arm for snake and trash_pickup and 3 for maze, and the speedrun comparison has 2 hint-free seeds.

## Related work and credits

- **Joseph Suarez and PufferLib** built the trainer, the Boxoban environment, the shaped configuration we compare against and the `simplecl` branch where this started.
- **DeepMind** made the [Boxoban levels](https://github.com/google-deepmind/boxoban-levels) and DRC ([Guez et al. 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1901.03559)).
- **@dkta0's ["Playing Sokoban backwards"](https://blog.dkta.dev/blog/playing-sokoban-backwards/)** also trains with a win-only reward, using a reverse curriculum. It plays real training levels backwards from the finished position, so games start one pull away from the goal and step further back as the agent improves. It reports 72.2% at 3B steps (reported on X; protocol not stated) [unverified: we could not open the X post].
- **The [Sokoban Speedrun](https://github.com/JeanKaddour/sokoban_speedrun)** non-LLM record is 74.2% on unfiltered test, greedy at 120 steps, in 12 min 38 s on one H100, with the shaped reward. Our 74.7% under the same protocol took 3B steps rather than 0.5B, so the two aren't a like-for-like race.

## Reproduce

Everything is in [inductiveML/sokoban-no-hints](https://github.com/inductiveML/sokoban-no-hints), including the trained checkpoint.

Score the shipped checkpoint on all splits. This takes a few minutes on one GPU and prints `REPRODUCED EXACTLY` when every level's result and action sequence matches the shipped files:

```bash
git clone https://github.com/inductiveML/sokoban-no-hints && cd sokoban-no-hints
scripts/setup.sh
scripts/eval.sh
```

Retrain from scratch:

```bash
scripts/setup.sh --trainer
scripts/train.sh b_ours
scripts/eval.sh runs/b_ours/ckpt/boxoban/b_ours/0000002999975936.bin my_b_ours
```
